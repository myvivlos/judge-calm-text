We put the kettle down without hurrying. Rain had been at the window for a while, soft as a hand turning pages, and the room had gone the colour of tea left to steep. Nobody said anything, and the quiet was not the empty kind, only the ordinary kind, a house settling into its own evening. Outside, the streetlight came on in stages, the way ice gives up and turns to water. Tomorrow would want answers. For now the clock was allowed to be slow, and the cat, having found the warmest chair, closed its eyes as though nothing here needed watching.
